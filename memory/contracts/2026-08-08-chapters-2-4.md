# A. Shared — everyone reads this

## A.1 Why this change exists

The simulator has one scenario: a mandazi stall. It teaches a learner to see their own
numbers, which is the right first job and, for a livelihood firm, close to the whole
job.

It is not the job this project exists to do. The owner asked for a deeper dive into
advanced business management across a progression — bakery, then factory, then export —
with a *simpler* interaction than today's. `AGENTS.md` §2 commits this project to
teaching things a stall cannot teach: financing an asset, managing working capital,
delegating with real authority, finding the one binding constraint, meeting a standard
someone else sets. Those only become real when the business is big enough to be
destroyed by getting them wrong.

**Read [`docs/arc.md`](../../docs/arc.md) before you start.** It contains the design, the
20-concept table for your chapter, and what each chapter is *for*. Your job is to turn
your chapter's table into playable content. Read [`AGENTS.md`](../../AGENTS.md) §2 and §6
too — they are short and they constrain what you write.

## A.2 Rules that bite

These are the ones this particular task is most likely to violate. Each has cost this
project real rework before.

**1. Do not invent regulatory detail.** `AGENTS.md` §6. No invented tax rates, licence
names, fee schedules, certification costs or thresholds. You will need many of these.
Where you need a specific number to make a decision meaningful, use one, mark the turn
`"unverified": true`, and add a line to your report naming exactly what needs checking.
Wrong specifics discredit the whole tool with the people it is meant to serve. This is
the single easiest way to fail this task.

**2. Content is data, never code.** No expressions, no formulas, no scripting in JSON.
The engine computes the economics; you supply decisions and their effects on state. If
you find yourself wanting to author an outcome number directly, you have misunderstood
the design — see the comment at the top of `app/js/engine.js`.

**3. Both languages, inline and key-major** (D-009). Every learner-facing string is
`{ "en": "...", "sw": "..." }`. Never a bare string. The Kiswahili is a first draft by
the same route that produced Q-015 and the app already shows a banner saying so — write
it anyway, in Tanzanian business register, and say in your report which word choices you
were least sure of. Mixing English loanwords the way business people actually speak is
correct; over-translating into pure Kiswahili reads as foreign.

**4. Every control must be able to reach its answer** (D-015). This is a
data-integrity rule, not a cosmetic one — session 007 shipped a prediction stepper that
could not reach the true answer on three of six numeric turns, and the behavioural record
scored those as the learner's misses. `validate-scenario.mjs` now walks for it. If it
fails on your chapter, the content is wrong, not the checker.

**5. No business-plan generation, no score, no rank** (ADR-0004). Nothing in your
content may tell a learner they are "ready", "suited to", "likely to succeed" or
"promising". Outcomes describe what happened; lessons describe what it means. That is
all.

**6. Write for a second-language reader.** Short sentences. Common words. Define a term
on first use, in the situation or the lesson — "working capital" means nothing to a
reader who has not been told what it is, and this is a teaching tool.

**7. Do not be cruel about livelihood businesses.** Q-006. Nothing may imply that
staying small is failure or that scaling is the point.

## A.4 The scenario file

Copy the top-level shape from `scenario-mama-asha.json`. Required keys: `id`, `version`,
`unverified`, `title`, `blurb`, `currency`, `startState`, `goal`, `recovery`, `turns`.
New keys are `carryIn` and, on a turn, `carryVariant` and `workout`.

### Turn shape

Identical to chapter 1's, with two additions:

```jsonc
{
  "id": "t07",
  "concept": "working-capital",
  "conceptLabel": { "en": "Money tied up", "sw": "Pesa iliyofungwa" },
  "scene": "stall-busy",
  "situation": { "en": "...", "sw": "..." },
  "workout": true,          // NEW, optional. See A.5.
  "advanceWeeks": 2,        // optional, as before
  "info": [ ... ],          // as chapter 1
  "diagnose": { ... },      // as chapter 1
  "carryVariant": {         // NEW, optional, at most TWO per chapter
    "flag": "keepsRecords",
    "when": true,
    "situation": { "en": "...", "sw": "..." }
  },
  "decision": { ... }       // as chapter 1: choice | number | allocate
}
```

`scene` must be one of exactly these nine, which are all `app/js/scene.js` draws:

```
stall-small  stall-busy  stall-empty  kitchen  market  office  truck  inspection  night
```

**Do not invent a scene name.** An unknown name does not error — it silently falls back
to drawing a mandazi stall, which in a factory chapter is worse than drawing nothing.
`validate-scenario.mjs` now rejects it. For chapters 2–4, `kitchen`, `office`, `truck`,
`inspection` and `market` will carry most of the load; adding scene art is not in your
slice.

### A.5 `workout` — the interaction simplification

The "work it out" step used to run on every turn. It now runs only where content asks
for it, plus automatically on any turn whose prediction is a number. This is most of what
"keep the interaction more basic" means in practice (D-017).

- **Do not** set `"workout": true` on more than **four** turns in your chapter.
- Set it where the learner genuinely needs the arithmetic in front of them before
  predicting — a break-even, a landed cost, a repayment they have to service.
- Everywhere else, leave it out. The common turn is
  `situation → decision → predict → reveal`.

### A.6 Decision types — use choice by default

You have exactly four, and **no new ones may be invented** (D-017).

| Type | Use it when | Budget for your chapter |
|---|---|---|
| `choice` | The default. 3 options. | ~13 turns |
| `number` | A number genuinely *is* the decision — a price, a quantity, a wage, an order size | **at most 6 turns** |
| `allocate` | Splitting a pot between competing uses | exactly 1 turn, the last one |
| `diagnose` | Read the ledger and name the line that caused something | 1–2 turns |

Advanced concepts are taught by **consequences in the ledger the learner already reads**,
not by new widgets. Working capital is taught by cash and profit visibly diverging in the
money panel — which the app now shows automatically whenever they differ — not by a
working-capital control.

### A.7 New state fields you can author and change

All of these already exist in `app/js/engine.js` with defaults that contribute nothing.
Read the comments there; they are the specification.

| Field | Meaning | In `effects` |
|---|---|---|
| `lines[]` | Product mix: `{ id, label, price, unitCost, demand, capacity }` | address one line as `"lines.bread.capacity": 200` — `demand`/`capacity` add, `price`/`unitCost` replace |
| `assetValue`, `assetLifeWeeks` | Equipment and its life. Depreciation = value ÷ life, charged weekly | `assetValue` adds; `assetLifeWeeks` replaces |
| `debt`, `interestRate`, `repayPerWeek` | Borrowing. Interest is a **cost**; repayment is **cash out and not a cost** | `debt` adds; the other two replace. `interestRate` is annual, as a fraction |
| `debtorWeeks`, `inventoryWeeks`, `creditorWeeks` | Working capital, in weeks. All replace. Clamped 0–26 | e.g. `"debtorWeeks": 4` |
| `payrollOnCost` | Statutory cost on top of wages, as a fraction. Replaces | `"payrollOnCost": 0.18` |
| `fxShare`, `fxRate`, `fxBase` | Share of revenue priced abroad, the rate, and the rate it was quoted at | all replace |
| `exportShare`, `dutyRate`, `freightPerUnit` | Landed cost, applied to the exported share only | all replace |
| `hoursPerCapacity`, `hoursPerStaff` | How the owner's week scales. **Already set in your startState — do not change these** | — |

**A currency move must be authored as a scheduled consequence** (`later`), never as
drift, so that when it arrives the learner is told which decision it followed. Every
delayed effect in this engine attributes itself; yours must too.

### A.8 `carryIn` — what the learner brought with them

Six flags, and the list is closed (ADR-0007): `keepsRecords`, `formality` (0–3),
`tookCredit`, `builtTeam`, `heldStandard`, `concentrated`.

```jsonc
"carryIn": [
  {
    "flag": "keepsRecords",
    "when": true,
    "startState": { "cash": 250000 },
    "note": { "en": "You arrived with six months of proper books.", "sw": "..." }
  },
  { "flag": "formality", "atLeast": 2, "startState": { "licenceFees": 40000 } }
]
```

Hard rules, all enforced by `validate-scenario.mjs`:

- A rule fires **only** when its flag is present. A learner who has played nothing
  before must get the authored `startState` untouched and **zero** opening notes.
- **Two to four `carryIn` rules.** They tint; they never gate.
- **At most two `carryVariant` turns** in the whole chapter.
- Your chapter must **set** the flags it wants to pass on, via ordinary `effects` on
  options — `"keepsRecords": true`, `"builtTeam": true`, `"tookCredit": true`. Look at
  which flags the next chapter reads and make sure yours can produce them.

### A.9 The goal

One `goal` spanning the run, 3 conditions, reported and never scored (D-013). Conditions
may use `field`, or `metric` — `weeksOfCostsCovered`, `weeklyProfit`, `weeklyCashFlow`,
`contributionMargin`, `cashCycleWeeks` — and either `min` (a floor) or `max` (a ceiling).
A shorter cash cycle is an achievement, so it wants `max`.

### A.10 The recovery chapter

Required. Copy the shape from chapter 1's `recovery`. It fires when cash goes below zero
and is the situation your chapter most needs to teach — trading out of a hole. Write it
for **your** business, not a copy of the stall's.

## A.11 Verification — run these, do not reason about them

From the repository root, in this order:

```bash
node scripts/validate-scenario.mjs app/content/<your file>   # must exit 0
node scripts/validate-i18n.mjs                               # must exit 0
node scripts/simulate-runs.mjs <your chapter id> -v           # must exit 0, and READ IT
node scripts/test-engine.mjs                                 # must stay at 194 passed
```

`simulate-runs.mjs` is the one that matters and the one you must actually read rather
than just check the exit code of. It plays your chapter four ways and prints cash,
profit, what reaches the bank, demand, capacity, reputation and the cash cycle. Look at
the numbers. `validate-scenario.mjs` checks band stability and reachability; it says
**nothing** about whether the business is viable, and it has missed two whole-business
failures in this project's history. Things to look for yourself:

- Does any strategy end with the business dead, or with cash in the tens of millions
  negative?
- Does weekly profit actually move, or is the whole run flat?
- For chapters 2–4: does the run ever show cash and profit differing? If not, your
  advanced content is being *narrated* rather than *modelled*, and the script warns you.
- Does the timid strategy survive? A chapter only the boldest path can finish is not a
  teaching tool.

# B. Shared — the house style

Chapter 1 is the reference. Match it. Concretely:

- **Situations are scenes, not exercises.** "A driver asks whether you could keep six
  aside for him each day, because he often arrives after you have sold out." Not "You
  must decide whether to offer a reservation service."
- **Options are things a person would actually do**, including the wrong ones, and none
  of them is labelled as the wrong one.
- **`outcome` says what happened.** `lesson` says what it means, in one or two sentences,
  and generalises beyond this turn. The lesson is the payload — write it first if it
  helps.
- **Never sneer at the cautious option.** A learner who declines a loan has made a
  defensible choice and the text must treat it as one.
- **Delayed consequences** (`later`) are how this project teaches that decisions have
  tails. Use them on **at least four** turns, each with a `cause` naming the decision, so
  the reveal can attribute it.
- **`info` items cost hours or cash and reveal something that actually changes the
  decision.** An info item that is merely flavour trains learners to ignore them — and
  information-seeking is one of the behaviours the record observes.

- `unitCost` on a line — turn 6, the wheat price move, arriving as a **scheduled**
  consequence with a `cause`.
- `staff`, `ownerHoursFixed` — turns 9 and 10. Hiring a manager who is not Asha must
  reduce her hours, and delegating by target rather than by method must be the option
  that scales.

**Flags this chapter must be able to emit:** `builtTeam` (turns 9–10), `heldStandard`
(turn 17), `concentrated` (turns 12–13), `tookCredit` (turn 16), `formality` (turn 14).

**`carryIn`:** read `builtTeam`, `tookCredit` and `keepsRecords` from chapter 2.

risk in the whole change.** Certificates of origin, phytosanitary requirements, HACCP,
ISO 22000, halal certification, customs procedure, preferential access arrangements —
you will need specifics for all of them and you must not invent any as fact. Use a
plausible figure, mark the turn `"unverified": true`, and list every single one in your
report. An exhaustive list here is worth more than polished content.
