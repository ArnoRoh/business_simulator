# MV-BS-TEST-001 — Learner acceptance protocol

**Status:** Prepared; not conducted. **Date:** 2026-09-08.
**Purpose:** Test whether intended learners can operate the game and explain its lessons.

## Participants and setting

Run two rounds with six adults per round. Include slower readers, people with little
phone experience, current owners and people without a current business. Start with
Kiswahili-speaking learners in Tanzania. Use their normal phones where possible, including
a low-end Android device with 2 GB RAM. Do not claim Ethiopian localisation from this test.

Explain the observation and obtain consent. Use synthetic game figures. Record no names,
phone numbers, identity documents or real business accounts in the repository. Participants
may stop at any time. Keep any identifiable recruitment records outside the public repo.

## Tasks

1. Open the game, choose a language and start the stall.
2. Explain the short situation, tap a price and describe what changed when the week ran.
3. Use help, explain cash versus profit, and finish the first five-decision episode.
4. Stop, close the browser, reopen it, and continue from the saved position.
5. Complete the cash-book exercise and explain the correction.
6. Turn off the connection and continue a downloaded chapter.
7. Use a chapter story transition, explain the next challenge, open the record and switch learners.
8. Explain a new example in which a profitable sale has not yet been paid.

Also record whether the learner notices the action buttons without prompting, understands
that a tap runs a week, uses help, and wants to continue after five choices. Ask which part
was interesting or frustrating. Check the reduced-motion setting. Do not treat completion
alone as evidence that the game is enjoyable.

The observer must not operate the controls. Record each point where verbal help was
needed, the exact unclear word or control, the learner's explanation and any lost work.
Measure elapsed time to discover friction; do not impose a time limit on the learner.

## Acceptance and second round

Target at least five of six completing episode one without someone operating the controls,
and five of six explaining the cash-versus-profit distinction in the new example.
Any lost record, inaccessible action or materially wrong calculation blocks acceptance.
Record results separately by language and reading/phone experience. Six participants are
not a representative sample and these thresholds do not establish business impact.

Fix observed problems after round one. Repeat with six new participants in round two.
Have a native speaker with business exposure review wording, and a local practitioner
review commercial figures. Keep the unverified labels until that review is complete.

## Portal acceptance when a prototype is available

**Status:** Prepared, not conducted. Apply the same two rounds of six adults, device,
language and consent rules above. Use synthetic application and report figures. Include
people with limited formal schooling, slower readers and little phone experience;
do not infer understanding from qualifications. Test permitted reading/typing help too.

1. Enter a test completion code and identify what to do next without an explanation.
2. Describe a business or a customer test, enter a money estimate, find an example,
   choose “I do not know yet”, and correct an answer using Back.
3. Save and leave, close the browser, reopen offline and find the same answers. Explain
   the difference between saved on the phone and sent to the programme.
4. Review the summary, explain who receives it, send and find confirmation. Interrupt
   the connection and check that retry does not require entering the answers again.
5. Open the report that is due. Identify its dates, enter two-month actuals, explain
   one difference from the forecast and give one action taken.
6. Complete the final reflection using earlier reports. Explain what went wrong and
   what changed, with a short answer rather than a written essay.
7. Explain which reading, translation and typing help is allowed, what AI use is
   prohibited, its consequence and how to respond to a mistaken finding.

Target five of six participants per round navigating entry, save/resume and reporting
without the observer operating controls, and five of six explaining dates, saved/sent
status and the assistance rule correctly. Observe short spoken/typed explanations;
record every unclear word and required prompt. These are proposed usability targets,
not evidence of business success or a validated selection threshold.

Lost answers, inaccessible controls, misleading submission status or money calculations
block acceptance. Test large text, screen-reader labels and interruptions on low-end
phones. Fix observed problems before the second round; do not certify ease of use from
a desktop screenshot or an automated test alone.
