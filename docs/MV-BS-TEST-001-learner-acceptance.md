# MV-BS-TEST-001 — Learner acceptance protocol

**Status:** Prepared; not conducted. **Date:** 2026-09-08.
**Purpose:** Test whether intended learners can operate the game and explain its lessons.

## Participants and setting

Run two rounds with six adults per round. Include slower readers, people with little
phone experience, current owners and people without a current business. Start with
Kiswahili-speaking learners in Tanzania. Use their normal phones where possible, including
a low-end Android device with 2 GB RAM. Do not claim Ethiopian localisation from this test.

Explain the observation and obtain consent. Use synthetic game figures. Record no names,
phone numbers, identity documents or real business accounts in the repository. Participants
may stop at any time. Keep any identifiable recruitment records outside the public repo.

## Tasks

1. Open the game, choose a language and start the stall.
2. Explain the situation in their own words, select an action, revise it, and run the week.
3. Use help, explain cash versus profit, and finish the first five-decision episode.
4. Stop, close the browser, reopen it, and continue from the saved position.
5. Complete the cash-book exercise and explain the correction.
6. Turn off the connection and continue a downloaded chapter.
7. Open the partial record, return to the game and switch to another local learner.
8. Explain a new example in which a profitable sale has not yet been paid.

The observer must not operate the controls. Record each point where verbal help was
needed, the exact unclear word or control, the learner's explanation and any lost work.
Measure elapsed time to discover friction; do not impose a time limit on the learner.

## Acceptance and second round

Target at least five of six completing episode one without someone operating the controls,
and five of six explaining the cash-versus-profit distinction in the new example.
Any lost record, inaccessible action or materially wrong calculation blocks acceptance.
Record results separately by language and reading/phone experience. Six participants are
not a representative sample and these thresholds do not establish business impact.

Fix observed problems after round one. Repeat with six new participants in round two.
Have a native speaker with business exposure review wording, and a local practitioner
review commercial figures. Keep the unverified labels until that review is complete.
