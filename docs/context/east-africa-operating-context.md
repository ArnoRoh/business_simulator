## Tanzania

### Formalisation

What must be established, per step: **what it is, what it costs, how long it takes in
practice, what it unlocks, and what happens if you skip it.**

- [ ] Business name registration
- [ ] Company incorporation, and when it is preferable to sole proprietorship
- [ ] Taxpayer registration
- [ ] Local government / municipal business licence
- [ ] Sector-specific licences and permits
- [ ] Premises requirements — health, safety, zoning
- [ ] Social security registration for employees
- [ ] The gap between the official process and the actual process, including realistic
      total elapsed time

### Tax

- [ ] Turnover thresholds and which regime applies at which level
- [ ] VAT registration threshold and what registration obliges
- [ ] Presumptive / small-taxpayer arrangements
- [ ] Employment-related taxes and levies
- [ ] Practical compliance burden: filing frequency, record-keeping, cost of a
      bookkeeper or accountant
- [ ] What non-compliance actually costs when it catches up

### Standards and certification

Frequently the real bottleneck. Each entry needs cost, lead time and what it unlocks.

- [ ] National standards and product certification, especially food
- [ ] Health and hygiene requirements for food handling
- [ ] Export requirements — certificates of origin, phytosanitary, destination-market
      requirements
- [ ] Organic and other voluntary certifications relevant to export value chains
- [ ] Testing and laboratory access — who, where, cost, turnaround
- [ ] Shared or public infrastructure available instead of building capability in-house

### Finance and payments

- [ ] Mobile money: providers, transaction costs at relevant volumes, float and
      settlement behaviour
- [ ] Bank account access for a small firm — requirements, realistic timelines
- [ ] Credit availability, actual rates, collateral demanded
- [ ] Supplier credit norms by sector
- [ ] Purchase-order and invoice finance — does it exist in practice, and for whom
- [ ] SACCOs and informal group finance
- [ ] Typical customer payment terms, and how long large buyers really take to pay

### Labour

- [ ] Minimum wage by sector, and typical actual wages
- [ ] Casual vs. permanent employment: legal distinction and practical usage
- [ ] Statutory obligations for employers
- [ ] Realistic cost of hiring a skilled technical employee or manager
- [ ] Availability of specialist skills — production engineering, quality, export

### Markets and value chains

- [ ] Value chains for the first scenario ([Q-005](../../memory/OPEN_QUESTIONS.md);
      honey leading) — structure, margins at each stage, who holds power
- [ ] Supermarket and institutional buyer requirements for supplier qualification
- [ ] Export market entry: buyers, agents, logistics, realistic costs
- [ ] Seasonality by sector — often decisive for cash flow and routinely under-modelled
- [ ] Transport and logistics costs, and their reliability

### Operating realities

- [ ] Electricity reliability and the cost of backup power
- [ ] Cold chain: availability and cost
- [ ] Internet and data costs at the level a small firm actually uses
- [ ] Storage and spoilage rates by product type

## How this feeds the simulation

Every cost and lead time here becomes a number in a scenario's data tables
([`../game-design.md`](../game-design.md), "Content model"). Scenarios are authored as
data precisely so that people with this knowledge and no programming skill can supply
and review it.

That means an error here propagates directly into what learners are taught. Treat these
tables as the highest-stakes content in the project.
