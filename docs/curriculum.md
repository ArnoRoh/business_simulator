# Curriculum: capability map

> **Status:** first draft, unreviewed, and **blocked on
> [Q-001](../memory/OPEN_QUESTIONS.md)** — the target learner segment. The shape below
> serves an existing small firm with some traction. If the primary learner turns out to
> be pre-revenue, much of this needs rewriting.

This is a capability map, not a syllabus. It says what a learner should be able to *do*.
How that is taught is [`game-design.md`](./game-design.md); what we observe is
[`assessment.md`](./assessment.md).

## Organising principle

Capabilities are grouped by the constraint they relieve, not by business-school subject.
There is no "Marketing 101" module, because learners do not experience a marketing
problem — they experience "nobody is buying at this price."

Each capability names the failure it prevents. If we cannot state the failure, the
capability does not belong.

---

## Track 0 — Orientation

Short, and it sets the frame for everything after.

**0.1 Which trajectory am I on?**
Livelihood consolidation and transformational growth are different paths with different
requirements. The learner chooses; the system never assigns. Handled carefully —
see [Q-006](../memory/OPEN_QUESTIONS.md).
*Prevents:* being taught scaling content by a tool that has assumed an ambition the
learner does not hold, or being quietly capped by one that assumed the opposite.

**0.2 What is my binding constraint?**
Named in the learner's own words at the start, revisited throughout.
*Prevents:* working on everything at once, which is the default and the most expensive
error.

## Track 1 — Money that is actually there

The most common cause of failure in an otherwise viable firm.

**1.1 Cash is not profit.** Timing, receivables, payment terms, the month you are
profitable and cannot pay wages.
*Prevents:* insolvency while trading profitably.

**1.2 Unit economics.** What one unit really costs, including the costs that hide —
transport, spoilage, mobile-money fees, financing, the owner's own time.
*Prevents:* scaling a loss.

**1.3 Pricing.** Cost-plus vs. value-based; what happens at each price; why the cheapest
seller in a commodity market has no defence.
*Prevents:* under-pricing, the single most common error in the target segment.

**1.4 Working capital vs. capability investment.** Money consumed on wages, rent and
stock vs. money that buys durable capacity.
*Prevents:* the failure mode the background note identifies in large-grant recipients —
hire, spend, contract when the subsidy runs out.

**1.5 Records that support decisions.** Enough bookkeeping to answer real questions;
not accounting for its own sake.
*Prevents:* deciding blind, and being unfundable because nothing can be verified.

## Track 2 — Customers before capacity

Built on the note's argument that demand validates a firm better than a pitch does.

**2.1 Who actually pays, and why.** Segments, willingness to pay, the difference between
interest and a purchase order.
*Prevents:* building for a customer who does not exist.

**2.2 Getting a paid trial.** Securing a real commitment — small, paid, specific —
before committing capital.
*Prevents:* scaling on assumption. Directly mirrors the execution test.

**2.3 Selling to organisations.** Procurement, supplier qualification, tenders, payment
terms, what a large buyer needs before they can buy from you at all.
*Prevents:* being locked out of the B2B and institutional demand that supports growth.

**2.4 Keeping customers.** Repeat business, consistency, what losing a large customer
does to a small firm.
*Prevents:* growth that leaks out of the bottom.

---

## Track 3 — Being a real business

Formalisation is expensive and slow. Modelled honestly, with real numbers, or not at
all. Country detail lives in [`context/`](./context/) — currently a **stub**.

**3.1 What formalisation actually costs.** Registration, licences, tax enrolment, in
money and months.
*Prevents:* the surprise that stalls firms halfway through, having paid for some of it.

**3.2 When to formalise.** It is a decision with timing, not an obligation with a
deadline. What it unlocks; what it costs to do too early.
*Prevents:* both premature formalisation and the informality ceiling.

**3.3 Standards and certification.** What a buyer, exporter or regulator requires;
lead times; why this is often the real bottleneck.
*Prevents:* discovering the requirement after the order is won.

**3.4 Tax and compliance in practice.** Thresholds, obligations, what non-compliance
costs later.
*Prevents:* liabilities that surface at the worst moment.

**3.5 Shared infrastructure.** Labs, calibration, cold storage, certification bodies —
capabilities to access rather than rebuild.
*Prevents:* each firm recreating the same expensive compliance capability alone.

## Track 4 — Building an organisation

Where transformational firms are made or lost. The characteristic failure is a founder
who cannot build beyond themselves.

**4.1 Hiring beyond family.** First non-family employees; what changes.
*Prevents:* the ceiling of who you happen to be related to.

**4.2 Delegation.** Handing over decisions, not just tasks. What it costs in the short
run and buys in the long run.
*Prevents:* the founder becoming the constraint — the central failure in the note.

**4.3 Systems and standard work.** Making output consistent without the owner present.
*Prevents:* quality collapsing exactly when volume arrives.

**4.4 Specialist capability.** When to bring in a production engineer, quality
specialist, export manager or finance lead — including fractionally.
*Prevents:* a technically-blocked firm trying to solve it with more effort.

**4.5 Making yourself removable.** The firm running without you.
*Prevents:* an unsellable, uninvestable, unscalable business.

---

## Track 5 — Bottlenecks and capability jumps

The synthesis track, and the closest thing to a thesis statement in the curriculum.

**5.1 Finding the binding constraint.** Which single thing, removed, changes the
trajectory — as opposed to the fifteen things that are all somewhat wrong.
*Prevents:* generic "business growth" spending.

**5.2 Verifying it.** Testing the claim "if X were removed, we could go from A to B"
before betting on it.
*Prevents:* confidently removing the wrong constraint.

**5.3 Costing the jump.** What the machine, certification, hire or test actually costs,
end to end.
*Prevents:* half-funded capability jumps, which are worse than none.

**5.4 Financing it.** Purchase-order finance, invoice discounting, recoverable grants,
supplier credit, customer prepayment — beyond "get a grant".
*Prevents:* growth that stalls waiting for the only funding instrument the learner knows.

**5.5 Sequencing.** Order of operations across several constraints under limited
capital.
*Prevents:* the right investments in an order that bankrupts you.

---

## Track 6 — When assumptions break

Not a module so much as a property of every scenario. Included because responding to
broken assumptions is one of the strongest indicators in the execution test.

Shock types: a major customer leaves; a supplier fails; a currency moves; a regulation
changes; a key employee leaves; a harvest fails; a competitor undercuts.

*Prevents:* nothing specific — this is where the learner shows what they are.

---

## Sequencing

Not linear. Track 0 first; Track 6 throughout. Tracks 1–5 are entered by scenario
demand: the learner meets a cash problem and gets Track 1 content when it is the thing
in front of them, not because it is week two.

Depth depends on the chosen trajectory. Livelihood consolidation goes deep on Tracks 1
and 2, lightly through 3, and barely into 4 and 5. Transformational growth needs all
six.
