# Code of Conduct

## Our commitment

This project builds a tool for entrepreneurs in East Africa, and it depends on
contributions from people with very different backgrounds — software developers,
educators, entrepreneurs, programme staff and researchers, working across languages,
countries and levels of formal education.

That mix is the point. It only works if people can contribute without being
condescended to.

## Expected behaviour

- Be direct about ideas and considerate toward people. Criticising a design is fine;
  criticising the person who proposed it is not.
- Assume competence. A contributor writing in a second language, or unfamiliar with a
  tool you use daily, is not less capable — and their knowledge of the operating context
  this project serves may exceed yours substantially.
- Value practical experience alongside technical and academic knowledge. Someone who has
  registered a business in Tanzania knows things no amount of reading supplies.
- Give feedback that says what to do, not just what is wrong.
- Accept correction. Being wrong about a fact is normal here — the subject matter is
  hard and much of it is unverified.

## Unacceptable behaviour

- Harassment, insults, or personal attacks
- Discriminatory language or conduct relating to nationality, ethnicity, gender,
  religion, age, disability, education, or economic circumstances
- Condescension toward contributors on the basis of language, technical fluency, or
  formal qualification
- Publishing others' private information without permission
- Sustained disruption of discussion
- Any conduct that would reasonably make someone withdraw from the project

## A specific note on the subject matter

This project deals with poverty, informal enterprise and the limits of development
programmes. Discussion of these topics is expected and welcome, including sharp
disagreement about what works.

But the people this tool serves are not an abstraction, and some contributors *are* those
people. Discussion of learners, entrepreneurs and programme participants should be
written as though they are reading it — because they may be. Language that treats them
as objects of study or as beneficiaries to be managed is not acceptable here.

## Scope

Applies in all project spaces — repository, issues, pull requests, discussions — and
when representing the project publicly.

## Reporting

Report concerns to the project maintainer at **arnorohwedder@gmail.com**.

Reports are handled confidentially. You will receive an acknowledgement, and the
maintainer will decide on a response — which may range from a private conversation to
removal from the project.

If your concern is about the maintainer, and you would rather not raise it directly,
raising it publicly in an issue is acceptable.

## Enforcement

The maintainer is responsible for clarifying standards and may edit, remove or reject
contributions, and temporarily or permanently ban contributors, for behaviour they judge
inappropriate. Where practical, an explanation will be given.
